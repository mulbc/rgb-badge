# First charger connections on the 114 mm study, 2026-10-14

The [separate KiCad routing trial](../../hardware/coupon/rev-a/staging/paired-charger-local-routing-trial-114mm.kicad_pcb) adds five local connections to the [114 mm footprint-only board](paired-charger-114mm-placement-study-2026-10-14.md): C53 to BQ24074 U41 BAT, C54 to U41 SYS, C56 to U41 IN, R92 to U41 ISET, and R93 to U41 ILIM. It also connects both J1 VBUS contacts to E0 U45 IN and C60, E0 OUT to E1 U44 IN and C61, and E1 OUT through C52 to U41 IN and C56. Thus the **three nets separated by E0/E1** have staged copper, while the eFuses themselves remain electrically unqualified. The C54/SYS path uses In3 copper; the input paths use In4 copper and standard 0.6/0.3 mm through vias. These are **geometry routes**, all 0.20 mm wide, not current-rated power copper or complete ground returns. The rest of the board remains unrouted. The proposed paired-row footprints and projected drivers remain netless.

Generate the trial using KiCad's bundled Python and [the deterministic source](../../tools/generate-paired-charger-114mm-routing.py). Two consecutive exports were byte-identical, SHA-256 `6bf274afdba4eed8f1b66f2e9312d66f8a2ba12d6bd04c7bc68891e48e6d2e18`. Native KiCad CLI 10.0.6 reports the same **104 intrinsic pad-gap clearance violations** as the unrouted parent and no new geometric findings; its [raw report](paired-charger-114mm-local-routing-drc-2026-10-14.json) still caps unconnected items at 499. The full KiCad ratsnest count falls from 1,494 to 1,482 open edges, twelve fewer, with 39 trace segments and ten through vias. The first straight BAT/ISET probe, first SYS-via position and first E1 output escape produced additional clearance findings; those paths were moved and the final trial rechecked. No hardware was built or measured.

This demonstrates that the added placement space permits charger support connections and the three USB-to-BQ rail segments without new DRC geometry errors. It does **not** establish eFuse behavior, capacitor ground returns, current capacity, thermal performance, row-stage correctness, battery/case fit or charging operation. Continue the power returns and current-rated copper on the chosen topology and review the actual loops and enclosure before treating this as a final board.
