## Calculation and implications

The existing BQ24074 ISET network has a calculated **0.698–0.872 A** charge-current range under the repo's retained programming-error assumptions. The 700 mAh GlobTek part is rated at no more than 0.700 A; the other two packs at no more than 0.400 A. Normal temperature regulation or input current limiting is not a valid substitute for setting a safe maximum charge current. Select the exact pack and protection/NTC evidence before choosing the final ISET resistor or claiming the 45–60-minute 80% goal.

`python3 tools/check-power-design.py --candidate-iset-ohm 1500 --pack-max-charge-ma 700` calculates **526–657 mA** and passes *only* the charge-current ceiling for the 700 mAh candidate. At 800 mAh / 400 mA, `--candidate-iset-ohm 2490 --pack-max-charge-ma 400` calculates approximately **317–396 mA** and passes that same limited check. Both resistor values are numerical screening targets, **not selected resistor MPNs or charger updates**. The current 1130-ohm ISET fails either candidate. The tool refuses missing, nonfinite or out-of-range inputs; it does not check thermistor trip temperatures or battery capacity.

The 45–60-minute 80% target in CHG-001 also needs screening independently of charger safety. Even assuming a constant maximum current from zero to 80% state of charge, no taper, no input/thermal limiting and no system load, the 800 mAh pack limited to 400 mA needs **at least 0.8 × 800 / 400 × 60 = 96 minutes**. The 2.49 kΩ proposal is slower still: its most optimistic 396 mA corner gives approximately **97 minutes**. Thus that exact pack cannot meet CHG-001's 60-minute upper target under the documented 400 mA charge limit. For the 700 mAh candidate, the 1.50 kΩ proposal gives an ideal **51–64-minute** range at its 657–526 mA calculated corners. A 60-minute result requires at least 560 mA *average battery charge current* over that interval; even the ideal constant-current arithmetic misses this at the 526 mA corner. Real precharge, CV taper, PowerPath load, thermal regulation and reduced usable capacity change observed timing, so neither candidate is a demonstrated charging-speed solution. If the 400 mA pack is eventually selected, formally revisit CHG-001 before claiming compliance; never raise its current to force the target.

Energy-only upper bounds at nominal capacity are **2.59 Wh / 6 h ≈ 432 mW** for the 700 mAh pack and **2.96 Wh / 6 h ≈ 493 mW** for an 800 mAh pack. These are gross power ceilings before converter losses, usable-depth limits, pack aging and any case/LED measurements. Neither number establishes six-hour runtime. The preliminary layout must include actual wrap and lead exit, mounting, swelling clearance and the three-pin keyed plug; nominal pouch dimensions alone are not a mechanical fit sign-off.

Using the existing [project-plan runtime model](../project-plan.md) rather than gross energy alone, the 35%-lit/25%-brightness reference case is approximately 0.45 W at the battery and assumes 85% usable nominal energy. The resulting **calculated** times are about **4.9 h for 700 mAh** and **5.6 h for 800 mAh**. The 700 mAh pack's documented capacity and discharge ceiling therefore make it a useful **coupon-only** dimensional/charger candidate, but it is not a demonstrated six-hour full-badge pack. Changing user-selected brightness or measured component efficiency could change these numbers; no automatic dimming is proposed.

There is a separate peak-load exclusion for the **full** badge: the same model estimates **2.49 W** at the battery for full-white brightness. At 3.0 V this implies **0.83 A** before transient and aging margins, exceeding the 700 mAh GlobTek pack's **0.70 A maximum continuous discharge**. Its PCM overcurrent threshold (1–4 A) does not enforce the lower continuous rating. Do not use that pack for an unrestricted full-badge build at the present display power. The coupon has only 16 columns and requires its own measured peak-current review; no final brightness or discharge claim follows from this screening. The 800 mAh GlobTek drawing specifies 1.4 A continuous, but its 400 mA charge maximum and 30 g mass remain major trade-offs.

As of this screening, DigiKey displayed 1,640 units of `BL0750F5030481S1PCTC` at $11.85 for one and an 18-week manufacturer standard lead time. Stock/price are snapshots, not reservations. Do not order now; an exact pack, revised charger setting and Gate A must precede the PCB release. The battery for an **isolated development test** can be chosen separately if its charger settings are explicitly compatible.

## Capacity floor and a near miss

Under the same provisional 0.45 W reference load, 85% usable energy and 3.7 V nominal voltage, six hours requires at least **0.45 W × 6 h / (3.7 V × 0.85) ≈ 859 mAh** nominal capacity. This is an arithmetic floor, not a pack specification: aging, voltage sag and a measured workload may demand more. Full-white screening separately requires at least **0.83 A continuous discharge at 3.0 V**, before peak margin. A pack's PCM trip threshold cannot replace its lower continuous rating.

Jauch `LP523450JU+PCM+MOLEX 51021-0300 70MM` is a US-distributor near miss. DigiKey lists 950 mAh, 34.5 mm pack width and a three-position connector. Jauch's catalogue gives **34.8 mm cell width**. That leaves only 0.2 mm inside the 35 mm *outside* case width even before walls, tolerances or swelling allowance. The manufacturer's available two-wire variant sheet states up to 1.0 A charge/discharge under specified temperature conditions, but it does not establish an NTC or the precise three-wire pack wiring. Exclude it from the current mechanical design rather than assuming that nominal capacity alone makes it a fit.
