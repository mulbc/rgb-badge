# Gauge I²C isolation screen, 2026-09-27

Status: engineering screen, **no bus switch selected or captured**. The [staged MAX17048 sheet](switch-gauge-boundary.md) connects its SDA/SCL directly to the shared application bus. An independent physical pole will remove its VDD/CELL, but the poles of the proposed DPDT slide switch have no guaranteed relative timing. During opening, `+3V3_APP` may still hold SDA/SCL high after gauge power disappears. During closing, the bus may rise before gauge supply. The MAX17048 [Rev. 7 datasheet](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/MAX17048-MAX17049.pdf) does not specify a powered-off I²C-pin leakage or back-power guarantee; its absolute-maximum input voltage is not one.

## Two-channel switch candidate screen

The [TI TMUX136 Rev. E datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tmux136.pdf) explicitly lists I²C compatibility and powered-off high-impedance signal paths. Its **VCC = 0 V** `IOFF` specification is nevertheless **±10 µA per signal pin at −40…+85 °C**, and **±50 µA per signal pin at −40…+125 °C** (electrical-characteristics tables, pages 5–6; signal pins at 1.65–3.45 V or 0–3.6 V). For two exposed SDA/SCL paths, even the +85 °C maxima would total **20 µA**, before protector, charger and regulator OFF leakage; the +125 °C two-pin sum would be **100 µA**. This is a deliberately conservative sum of *per-pin* guaranteed bounds, not a measurement or an expected typical drain. With the presently documented 6.5 µA charger no-input limit, the +125 °C sum alone exceeds the <50 µA requirement, and the +85 °C case leaves at most 23.5 µA for everything else. Neither figure proves a topology is impossible: in a settled hardware OFF state the application pull-ups should be unpowered, and whether the switch pins remain exposed to another source depends on the final wiring.

The same device consumes up to **70 µA with VCC = 4.8 V over −40…+125 °C** in its normal switch state; even its EN-disabled high-impedance mode has a **20 µA maximum** at that condition (page 7). A battery-fed, always-on mux would therefore lack a comfortable *guaranteed* OFF-current margin; a switched-rail-fed mux needs additional proof for its own rail-decay and control-pin sequence. Its published operating conditions require **2.3–4.8 V VCC**, **0–3.6 V analog I/O**, and a **100–1000 µs/V VCC ramp** (page 4), which must be reconciled with the selected source and contact bounce. These are datasheet-bound screens, not proposed connections.

[TI TMUX1136](https://www.ti.com/product/TMUX1136) advertises exceptionally low **on/off** leakage while powered, but its product description guarantees fail-safe behavior for its *logic controls*, and its signal range is from GND to VDD. Those properties do not establish a powered-off SDA/SCL isolation guarantee. Do not equate powered ON switch-off leakage with power-off `IOFF`.

## Decision boundary

Keep the simple two-pole hardware switch and direct gauge bus **only as the present draft**. Before electrical capture of the physical switch, resolve transient I²C exposure through manufacturer-qualified powered-off behavior, a topology that disconnects both bus lines before the gauge loses power with proven sequencing, or a bounded protective design with all rail/temperature/leakage corners. No firmware action can guarantee safe contact timing when the user moves a physical switch. The finished circuit must also keep charger autonomy in OFF, prove <50 µA after settling at supported temperatures, meet I²C rise/LOW timing, and fit the enclosure. Bench testing can corroborate a design but does not turn a typical-only leakage value into a worst-case guarantee.
