# Coupon Rev A 3.3 V application converter capture contract

Status: **pre-capture engineering calculation, 2026-09-30**. No 3.3 V converter circuit has been placed in the canonical KiCad schematic, no complete input rail or physical switch is connected, and no board has been built. This increment does not select the battery, charger ISET, or a PCB order. It applies the hard-OFF decision in [ADR 0004](../../../docs/decisions/0004-standalone-charging-and-hard-off-state.md), the independently switched gauge in [ADR 0015](../../../docs/decisions/0015-switch-fuel-gauge-with-application.md), and PWR-003/004 in [requirements](../../../docs/requirements.md).

## Circuit boundary and defaults

| TPS631000DRLR pin | Planned net or connection | Capture condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 VOUT | `+3V3_APP` | Output capacitor and divider upper resistor return here; do not connect to USB-only 3.3 V. |
| 2 LX2, 3 LX1 | Opposite ends of the 1 µH inductor | Short, wide switching loop per TI layout guidance; no global power labels on LX. |
| 4 VIN | Protected BQ24074 charger `OUT` / `SYS` after its voltage/permission design is completed | Input capacitor to local ground. Never connect to the gauge's `+BAT_GAUGE_SW` pole. The BQ24074 specifies 4.3–4.5 V regulated OUT with a sufficient USB input; battery-only OUT is supplied through the BAT FET. Bound switching/fault transients separately within the converter's 1.6–5.5 V operating range. |
| 5 EN | The application pole of the latching ON/OFF switch drives it high from a valid protected source; an independent resistor pulls it to GND in OFF and throughout an open/bouncing contact. | Switch contact may drive *only control current*, not application load. Candidate 100 kΩ pull-down needs an exact resistor MPN, contact and OFF-leakage audit; it is not yet captured. Do not rely on MCU GPIO or an internal pull-down. |
| 6 MODE | GND | Fixed auto-PFM default for light-load efficiency; do not float or make boot behavior firmware-dependent. |
| 7 GND | Local power return | Tie input/output capacitors and feedback lower resistor into a suitable ground layout. |
| 8 FB | Junction of 511 kΩ VOUT-to-FB and 91 kΩ FB-to-GND | Keep the sense node clear of LX and high-current traces. |

The gauge uses a **separate** switch pole for its cell feed. VLED needs the physical ON condition **and** a hardware-qualified display request, with VLED disabled at boot/reset/programming. Sharing an ON-permission signal with the LED interlock is a later reviewed circuit decision, not a reason to route LED current through the switch. When OFF, the 3.3 V converter must disconnect its output and the unpowered application must not be back-fed from USB data, gauge I²C, debug pads, or another rail. TI calls the TPS631000 behavior *true shutdown with load disconnect*, but whole-board OFF isolation and <50 µA remain measured requirements. The separately powered USB-only logic is allowed to operate when USB is attached while the application switch is OFF.

With TI's 500 mV nominal feedback reference, the nominal output is `0.500 × (1 + 511/91) = 3.3077 V`. An **illustrative, independent ±1% initial tolerance** on both resistors combined with TI's 495–505 mV reference limits gives `3.2196–3.3981 V`. For an illustrative PCB resistor excursion of **60 °C from its reference temperature**, allowing the two ±100 ppm/K TCRs to oppose each other adds ±0.6% drift *per resistor*; together with initial tolerance and the same feedback limits, the calculated range is `3.1871–3.4330 V`. This is a sensitivity screen, not a guaranteed delivered voltage or an assertion of the final PCB temperature: long-term drift, loading, routing drop, ripple and transients require a full worst-case check. Divider standing current is about `3.3077 V / (511 kΩ + 91 kΩ) = 5.49 µA` while ON; this divider belongs entirely on the switched output, so it cannot drain the battery through an enabled output in hard OFF.

**Consumer voltage screen:** The [ESP32-S3-WROOM-1U module](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-s3-wroom-1_wroom-1u_datasheet_en.pdf) requires 3.0–3.6 V at its supply pads; the [TLC59581](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tlc59581.pdf) specifies 3.0–5.5 V VCC; the [TS3USB31E application USB switch](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ts3usb31e.pdf) specifies 2.25–4.3 V VCC; the [INA232](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ina232.pdf) specifies 1.7–5.5 V; and [74HC4514](https://www.nexperia.com/product/74HC4514PW) specifies 2.0–6.0 V. These are the principal active loads identified so far, not a complete pad-by-pad audit. The illustrative static extremes leave only **187 mV below** the module's 3.0 V minimum and **167 mV above** the static 3.433 V maximum before reaching its 3.6 V upper limit. TI's 150 mV response to a 1 A load step is a *typical* advertised result, not a guaranteed maximum or a license to spend almost all 167 mV. Actual droop/overshoot at the module, RF/BLE bursts, display transitions, startup and charger/source steps must remain within every load's limits over component and temperature corners. If that cannot be shown, revisit the feedback tolerance/value or capacitance rather than accepting a marginal rail.

The candidate 100 kΩ EN pull-down, if connected as specified and if **only** the published ±0.3 µA maximum EN input bias at 5.5 V opposes it, develops at most 30 mV in OFF; TI lists a 0.5 V minimum falling threshold. This is a component-level margin illustration, **not** a bound on switch contamination, PCB leakage, an attached debug tool, or an upstream driver. In ON it draws about 27–43 µA from a 2.7–4.3 V control source; the closed switch contact must keep EN above TI's 1.2 V rising threshold at all valid SYS voltages. The pull-down must return to GND, never to unswitched SYS.

The 80% column applies the catalog ±20% initial capacitance tolerance *as a sensitivity illustration* to the characteristic curve; it is not a validated combined worst-case model. The [BQ24074 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/bq24074.pdf), §8.5, specifies **4.3–4.5 V** regulated OUT for this exact variant when the input has sufficient headroom; §9.3.4 describes lower OUT voltage under DPPM/supplement and battery-only supply. Thus 5.5 V normal USB VBUS does **not** imply 5.5 V steady converter VIN. At 4.5 V the CIN illustration leaves 0.969 µF before temperature and aging. As a further *illustrative*, independent 15% reduction for X5R temperature variation gives 4.394 µF CIN at 4.5 V and 10.970 µF COUT at the illustrative 3.433 V rail high: only 0.194 and 0.570 µF respectively before aging and other effects. These multiplications are sensitivity screens, not guaranteed lower bounds. A single capacitor in either position is still a candidate, not qualified; parallel capacitance is one compact option to assess after the actual board/charger fault and transient envelope is defined. At the converter's 5.5 V operating input boundary the single CIN illustration misses the minimum by 0.095 µF even *before* the extra temperature term, but that input voltage is a fault/transient scenario, not the BQ24074's regulated steady OUT. Verify the final SYS transient maximum, exact lot curves, temperature, aging and assembled rail behavior before release.

TI specifies >1.65 V for startup, but its 1.5 A example and front-page output-current claim require VIN ≥2.7 V at 3.3 V output. Do not infer full-load operation down to converter UVLO or the cell protector's cutoff. Confirm minimum `SYS` at the final protected-pack end of discharge and while source qualified charging is present, with simultaneous ESP32/radio/logic loads. TI's typical example output current is a **capability condition**, not a measured badge load or efficiency promise.

**Inductor first-order screen:** Applying TI's boost-mode Equation 3 to the *example* 1.5 A output at VIN 2.7 V, VOUT 3.3 V, assumed 90% efficiency, 0.8 µH (the candidate's nominal 1 µH minus its initial 20% tolerance), and 1.8 MHz (TI's stated minimum switching frequency) gives `D = 0.1818`, average inductor current `2.037 A`, half ripple `0.170 A` and `Ipeak ≈ 2.207 A`. TI's suggested 20% saturation margin would require `≥2.649 A` against Murata's **4.3 A 30%-drop** figure at the specified reference condition. The separate **3.2 A / +40 °C** criterion is not a board thermal result; its winding rise, local ambient, inductance under DC bias, and actual peak current must be qualified in the small enclosure. Efficiency was assumed, not measured. A pack/charger SYS below 2.7 V or a higher load needs a fresh calculation.

## Next capture and release checks

1. Audit exact capacitor/inductor manufacturer drawings and effective-capacitance curves, then create their project-local symbols/land patterns. Complete native review of the staged feedback symbols and recheck the ERJ2 manufacturer land drawing. Check the inductor-current margin against TI's worst operating-condition equation and derating, not only its table saturation value. Compare the output tolerance screen with every 3.3 V consumer's guaranteed range.
2. Finish the protected `SYS` source and exact two-pole switch (including orientation/contact sequencing), then wire VIN and EN. The USB-only rail must remain distinct. Do not make a draft PWR_FLAG look like a real `SYS` source.
3. Capture a dedicated schematic sheet, extend exported-netlist invariants to the eight IC pins, feedback network, switched enable, MODE, caps, inductor and output loads; require zero configured ERC. Inspect native KiCad 10.0.6 PDF and footprint layers. Replace/reconcile old draft supply flags at the real source boundary.
4. Before Gate A, calculate tolerance/ripple/derating and startup/inrush over minimum pack voltage, charging and 5.5 V input envelope; review hot loops, thermal copper, EMI and assembled thickness. On the coupon, log the rail **at the ESP32-S3 pads** during switch bounce, USB attach/detach, RF bursts, simultaneous LED switching and output load steps, including 3.0 V minimum and 3.6 V maximum acceptance checks with instrument bandwidth/probe method recorded. Measure OFF rail voltage and battery current both before firmware has ever run and after normal use, with USB absent and present separately.
